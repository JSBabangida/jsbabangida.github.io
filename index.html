About me

I am an economist and PhD candidate at Griffith University, Australia, specialising in development economics, labour economics, and applied microeconomics. My research examines how conflict, climate shocks, and economic disruptions shape labour markets, household livelihoods, migration, and food security, particularly in developing economies. I use causal inference, econometric modelling, household survey data, and geospatial analysis to investigate these relationships and inform evidence-based policy.

My research has been published in journals including Applied Economics, Journal of Policy Modeling, Economies, and Letters in Spatial and Resource Sciences. Beyond my doctoral research, I have contributed to projects on labour-market transitions, migration, road-safety economics, and policy evaluation.

I have academic teaching and research experience across Griffith University, the University of Queensland, and Ahmadu Bello University, Nigeria. My teaching covers microeconomics, econometrics, quantitative methods, and international trade. My broader research interests centre on understanding economic vulnerability and identifying policies that improve livelihoods, employment opportunities, and economic resilience.

Research

My projects connect rigorous empirical work with questions facing policymakers, communities and institutions.

Conflict & livelihoods

Violent conflict, employment and displacement

Research on how violence affects household livelihoods, employment, coping strategies and forced displacement, with evidence from Nigeria.

Labour mobility

Olympic hosting and regional labour adjustment

Working-paper research examining Olympic hosting as a labour-demand shock and its implications for worker mobility and regional adjustment.

Food security & climate

Household exposure to shocks

Research on food insecurity, flooding, climate vulnerability and migration, drawing on household and spatial data.

Transport policy

Economic evaluation of road-safety interventions

Cost-benefit research on two-tier drug-driving enforcement and school-zone road-conflict interventions undertaken at The University of Queensland.

Political economy

Criminal markets and persistent violence

Working-paper research on social normalisation, organisational capital and the persistence of criminal activity in Nigeria.

Impact evaluation

Policy and programme evaluation

Research and technical support for field studies, labour-market interventions and monitoring frameworks, including active school travel.
